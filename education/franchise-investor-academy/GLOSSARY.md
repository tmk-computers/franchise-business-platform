# Franchise Investor Terminology Glossary
## Franchise Investor Academy — Comprehensive Reference Lexicon

**Version:** `1.0.0`  
**Classification:** Reference Reference Guide  
**Cross-Reference:** [Platform Glossary](../../docs/GLOSSARY.md)  

---

## A

### Area Development Franchise
A commercial franchising agreement where the franchisee purchases the exclusive right to develop and open a specified number of franchise locations within a defined geographic territory over an agreed-upon timetable.

### Assured Return / Guaranteed Return (Red Flag)
A deceptive marketing claim where a franchisor promises an investor a fixed percentage return on capital regardless of actual store performance. In India, such promises often trigger regulatory scrutiny under SEBI Collective Investment Scheme regulations and consumer protection laws. Legitimate business models do not guarantee profits.

### Arbitration Clause
A contractual provision specifying that any commercial disputes between franchisor and franchisee will be settled through binding arbitration rather than conventional court litigation, commonly governed in India by the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

---

## B

### Basket Size / Average Order Value (AOV)
The average monetary amount spent by a customer per single transaction. Crucial for unit economics modeling: Gross Revenue = Transaction Volume $\times$ Average Basket Size.

### Break-Even Point (Operational)
The exact volume of sales (in units or currency) required during a monthly operating cycle to cover all fixed and variable operating costs, yielding zero profit and zero loss:
$$\text{Break-Even Units} = \frac{\text{Fixed OpEx}}{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost Per Unit}}$$

---

## C

### Capital Expenditures (CapEx)
The upfront funds required to build, furnish, equip, and license a new franchise outlet before opening its doors. Includes interior fit-out, kitchen/IT hardware, signage, initial licensing, and initial security deposits.

### Catchment Area
The geographic zone surrounding a commercial retail location from which the majority of its footfall, customers, or client inquiries originate (typically measured as 1 km, 3 km, or 5 km radiuses, or 10-minute drive times).

### COCO (Company Owned, Company Operated)
A retail format where the franchisor owns 100% of the physical asset, incurs all capital expenditure, employs all personnel directly, and retains 100% of operational profits. Used by brands to test market demand before franchising.

### COFO (Company Owned, Franchise Operated)
A model where the parent enterprise owns the leasehold and capital assets of the facility, but contracts an independent franchise operator to manage staffing and daily operations in return for a commission or profit share.

---

## D

### Deal Registration Lock
A protective policy within B2B and software franchises where a franchisee logs a prospective enterprise lead in a centralized CRM. Once logged, other franchisees and the parent company are barred from soliciting that client for a predetermined exclusivity window (typically 90 days).

### Discovery Day
A structured meeting where a prospective franchisee visits the franchisor's corporate headquarters, meets executive management, inspects company-operated units, and completes mutual cultural and financial evaluations before signing agreements.

### Due Diligence
The comprehensive quantitative, operational, and legal audit conducted by a prospective investor before signing a franchise contract or remitting upfront capital.

---

## E

### Encroachment
The cannibalization of an existing franchisee's sales volume caused by the franchisor opening another company unit or granting another franchise location in close geographic proximity. Prevented through clear territory exclusivity clauses.

### Escrow Settlement Account
A specialized bank account governed by a tripartite agreement (franchisor, franchisee, and scheduled commercial bank) that holds customer receipts and automatically splits funds into franchisee and franchisor accounts based on contractual revenue-sharing percentages.

---

## F

### Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD)
A formal pre-sale disclosure document mandated in jurisdictions like the United States (by the FTC) that contains 23 standardized items covering executive history, litigation, fees, turnover, and audited financial statements. While not legally mandated as a specific statutory form in India, institutional franchisors provide equivalent Commercial Disclosure Packs.

### FOFO (Franchise Owned, Franchise Operated)
The most prevalent franchise archetype in India. The franchisee funds 100% of the setup capital and manages daily store operations, staffing, and local sales. The franchisor provides brand licensing, technology, central supply chain, training, and marketing support in exchange for upfront fees and royalties.

### FOCO (Franchise Owned, Company Operated)
A franchise structure where the investor provides the capital and real estate, but the franchisor assumes complete responsibility for operating the unit (hiring staff, managing inventory, running daily sales). Profits are split based on a predetermined ratio (e.g. 50:50).

---

## G

### Goods and Services Tax (GST)
A multi-stage indirect consumption tax levied across India (typically 18% for software, professional services, and franchise royalties; 5% or 12% for select food/retail categories). **Critical Rule:** In franchise financial models, statutory GST must be segregated immediately as a pass-through tax before computing commissions or royalties.

### Gross Billing vs. Net Realized Revenue
Gross Billing is the total tax-inclusive amount paid by the customer. Net Realized Revenue is the remaining capital after deducting statutory GST and customer discounts. Royalties must strictly be applied against Net Realized Revenue.

---

## I

### Initial Franchise Fee
A one-time, non-refundable upfront payment made by the franchisee upon signing the agreement, granting the license to use the brand name, operating manual, proprietary software, and initial onboarding training.

### Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
The annualized effective compounded return rate that makes the net present value (NPV) of all cash flows (initial investment plus subsequent monthly distributions) equal to zero. A superior metric to simple payback because it accounts for the time value of money.

---

## J

### J-Curve (Working Capital)
The graphical trajectory representing a new business's cumulative cash flow over its initial 6 to 18 months. Because initial setup costs and operating losses occur before break-even is attained, cash reserves dip into negative territory (the base of the "J") before rising into sustained profitability.

---

## M

### Master Franchise
A commercial arrangement where the franchisor grants an individual or corporate entity exclusive territorial rights to an entire state, region, or country. The master franchisee operates flagship units and acts as a regional franchisor, sub-franchising to local operators and sharing royalties with the global brand.

### Multi-Unit Franchise
An arrangement where a single franchisee signs an agreement to purchase, open, and operate multiple units within a specified geographic territory over a multi-year horizon.

---

## N

### Non-Compete Covenant
A contractual restriction preventing the franchisee from owning, managing, or consulting for a competing business during the term of the franchise agreement and for a reasonable period following termination within a defined geographic radius (subject to Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872).

---

## O

### Operating Expenses (OpEx)
The ongoing recurring costs required to operate a franchise unit on a monthly basis, including rent, employee wages, electricity, broadband, local marketing, inventory replenishment, and software subscriptions.

---

## P

### Payback Period
The length of time required for the cumulative net monthly cash inflows of a franchise unit to equal the total upfront capital invested (CapEx plus initial working capital cushion).

---

## R

### Royalty Fee / Revenue Share
The ongoing payment made by the franchisee to the franchisor (typically weekly or monthly) for continued brand usage, ongoing product engineering, central server maintenance, and operational guidance. Formatted as either a percentage of Net Revenue (e.g., 5% to 8%) or a fixed recurring subscription fee.

---

## S

### Sunk Cost
Upfront capital expenditure or onboarding fees that have already been spent and cannot be recovered if the business closes. Investors must distinguish recoverable security deposits from sunk onboarding costs.

---

## T

### Taluka / Tehsil Boundary
A sub-district administrative unit in India comprising urban centers and surrounding rural clusters. Frequently utilized as the primary exclusive territory boundary in regional and B2B franchise expansion models.

### Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)
A statutory mechanism in India where the entity paying for contractual services must withhold a predetermined percentage of tax (e.g. under Section 194C or 194J of the Income Tax Act) and deposit it directly with the central government.

---

## W

### Working Capital Cushion
The liquid reserve fund maintained by a franchisee to cover monthly fixed operating deficits (rent, payroll, utilities) before the business achieves operational break-even, typically sized for 3 to 6 months of zero-sales velocity.
