## About This Book

The most common financial catastrophe in first-time franchise investing is not operating at a loss—it is running out of cash while building out the business or waiting for footfalls to ramp up. Franchise sales brochures routinely obscure this danger by presenting simplified "Return on Investment (ROI)" percentages that conflate operating profit with cash payback, omit statutory GST pass-throughs, and ignore essential working capital buffers.

**Franchise Unit Economics & Financial Literacy** equips prospective investors with the mathematical toolkit required to analyze a franchise opportunity like an institutional private equity analyst. It explains the distinction between one-time Capital Expenditures (CapEx) and recurring Operating Expenses (OpEx), teaches you how to construct realistic 36-month pro-forma cash flow forecasts, isolates pass-through taxes (18% GST), and demonstrates how to stress-test unit break-even points under pessimistic operating scenarios.

---

## Learning Objectives

Upon reading this book, you will be able to:
1. **Deconstruct Total Investment:** Itemize and budget CapEx, sunk onboarding fees, working capital reserves, and contingency buffers.
2. **Isolate Pass-Through Taxes:** Correctly calculate net distributable revenue by removing 18% GST from gross point-of-sale customer receipts.
3. **Calculate Operational Break-Even:** Determine exactly how many transactions or rupees of daily revenue are required to cover fixed overhead.
4. **Distinguish ROI from Simple Payback:** Understand how many months of post-tax operating cash flow are required to return 100% of invested capital.
5. **Execute a Zero-Revenue Stress Test:** Determine how many months your business can survive under macro disruptions or prolonged footfall droughts.
