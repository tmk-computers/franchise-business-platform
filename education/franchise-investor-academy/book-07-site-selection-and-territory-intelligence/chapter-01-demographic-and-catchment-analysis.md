## 1. The Three Catchment Tiers

A retail or food service business draws customers from three distinct geographic layers:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             THE 3-TIER CATCHMENT MODEL                                |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                       |
|   TERTIARY CATCHMENT (3.0 km - 5.0 km / 15-20 min transit)                            |
|   - Contributes 10% - 15% of total sales.                                             |
|   - Destination shoppers, weekend visitors, online delivery aggregators.              |
|                                                                                       |
|       SECONDARY CATCHMENT (1.5 km - 3.0 km / 5-10 min transit)                        |
|       - Contributes 20% - 25% of total sales.                                         |
|       - Occasional visitors, suburban commuters, weekend diners.                     |
|                                                                                       |
|           PRIMARY CATCHMENT (0.0 km - 1.5 km / 5-8 min walking)                       |
|           - Contributes 60% - 70% OF TOTAL REVENUE.                                   |
|           - Immediate residential households, office workers, local students.        |
|           - High frequency, repeat footfall base.                                     |
|                                                                                       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Analyzing the Primary Catchment (The 60–70% Rule)
The **Primary Catchment** is the single most critical determinant of unit survival:
- In urban India, pedestrian walking tolerance is limited to 500 to 1,000 meters due to weather, traffic congestion, and sidewalk conditions.
- If your primary catchment does not contain your target demographic (e.g., dual-income households with household income >₹15 Lakhs for premium cafes, or students for value QSRs), destination traffic from further away will rarely save the unit.

## 2. The 4-Day Manual Footfall Audit Protocol

Franchisors often present high-level municipal traffic counts claiming *"50,000 people pass this street daily."* Prospective investors must verify what proportion of those 50,000 are actual walking consumers within their target demographic.

### The Field Audit Schedule
Stand directly outside the proposed storefront and record pedestrian passerbys across **4 distinct days**:
- **2 Weekdays:** Tuesday (baseline normal trade) and Thursday (pre-weekend momentum).
- **2 Weekend Days:** Saturday (family shopping) and Sunday (evening leisure).

### The 4 Dayparts Audit Intervals
Record counts during four critical 60-minute windows:
1. **08:30 – 09:30 (Morning Commute):** Measures office and school footfalls; assesses breakfast/coffee potential.
2. **12:30 – 13:30 (Lunch Peak):** Measures corporate office lunch traffic and midday shoppers.
3. **17:30 – 18:30 (Evening Transition):** Measures students, commuters returning home, evening snackers.
4. **20:30 – 21:30 (Dinner Peak):** Measures dine-in families, couples, and evening leisure footfall.

## 3. Modeling Ground-Truth Revenue from Field Data

Once raw pedestrian counts are collected, apply realistic retail conversion benchmarks to estimate daily transaction volume.

| Retail Vertical | Target Passerby Walk-In Rate | In-Store Conversion Rate | Overall Net Conversion Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quick-Service Restaurant (QSR)** | 8.0% – 12.0% | 85.0% – 95.0% | **7.0% – 10.0%** |
| **Boutique Cafe / Bakery** | 4.0% – 7.0% | 75.0% – 85.0% | **3.0% – 5.5%** |
| **Fashion / Apparel Retail** | 5.0% – 9.0% | 20.0% – 35.0% | **1.5% – 3.0%** |
| **Pharmacy / Convenience Store** | 10.0% – 15.0% | 90.0% – 98.0% | **9.0% – 14.0%** |
| **Optical / Specialty Retail** | 2.0% – 4.0% | 30.0% – 50.0% | **0.8% – 1.8%** |

### Mathematical Formula for Modeled Daily Net Sales:

$$\text{Daily Net Transactions} = \text{Daily Target Footfall} \times \text{Net Conversion Rate}$$

$$\text{Estimated Monthly Net Sales} = \text{Daily Transactions} \times \text{Local Average Order Value (AOV)} \times 30$$

### Numerical Example:
- **Observed Daily Target Footfall:** 2,400 pedestrians
- **Retail Concept:** Boutique Cafe
- **Net Conversion Rate:** 3.5%
- **Expected Daily Transactions:** $2,400 \times 0.035 = 84 \text{ transactions/day}$
- **Local AOV (ex-GST):** ₹160.00
- **Daily Net Sales:** $84 \times ₹160 = ₹13,440.00$
- **Estimated Monthly Net Sales:** $₹13,440 \times 30 = ₹4,03,200.00$

If the franchisor's sales deck promised monthly sales of ₹12,00,000 for this exact location, your empirical field audit has just revealed a massive 66% shortfall before you committed a single rupee!

## 4. The Rent-to-Revenue Affordability Threshold

No matter how attractive a commercial retail property looks, its rent must remain strictly within sustainable vertical thresholds. Paying excessive rent guarantees that all operating profits accrue to the commercial landlord rather than the franchise investor.

$$\text{Occupancy Cost Ratio} = \frac{\text{Base Rent} + \text{CAM Charges} + \text{Property Taxes}}{\text{Net Realized Revenue}} \times 100$$

### Industry Affordability Thresholds:

```
OCCUPANCY COST RATIO (%)
0%           10%          15%          20%          25%
[-------------|------------|------------|------------]
  SAFE ZONE     ACCEPTABLE    DANGER ZONE   INSOLVENCY
  (<10%)        (10% - 15%)   (15% - 20%)   (>20%)
```

- **Food & Beverage (QSR/Cafe):** Maximum sustainable occupancy cost is **12% to 15%** of net sales. Exceeding 18% leaves zero margin to cover 32% food costs, 18% labor, and 6% royalties.
- **Fashion / Apparel Retail:** Maximum sustainable occupancy cost is **15% to 18%**.
- **Pre-Schools / Daycare:** Maximum sustainable occupancy cost is **18% to 22%** (due to high carpet area requirements and 80%+ gross margins).

## 5. Investor Warning Signs in Real Estate Selection

> [!WARNING]
> ### 🚨 Commercial Real Estate Red Flags
> 1. **"One-Way Vehicular Flyover" Trap:** A store located on an arterial road directly beneath a newly constructed elevated flyover. Traffic moves at 60 km/h with zero vehicular turning access or parking availability.
> 2. **Narrow Frontage / Invisible Facade:** A shop with deep carpet area (80 feet) but an extremely narrow storefront (10 feet) obscured by tree branches, transformer boxes, or bus shelters.
> 3. **Unchecked CAM Charges:** A lease where Common Area Maintenance (CAM) charges are undefined or escalate at 15% annually at the landlord's unilateral discretion.

## 6. Key Takeaways

1. **Verify Footfall Independently:** Never trust municipal vehicle counts or franchisor sales estimates. Count pedestrians manually across 4 days and 4 dayparts.
2. **Focus on Primary Catchment:** 60% to 70% of your sales will come from people living or working within an 8-minute walking radius.
3. **Enforce the 15% Occupancy Ceiling:** In F&B and retail, if total rent and CAM exceed 15% of your realistic net sales projection, walk away from the property.
4. **Frontage Drives Walk-Ins:** A store with wide glass frontage on the natural pedestrian walking side of the street outperforms a larger store on the opposite side.

## 7. Chapter Knowledge Check

1. **What percentage of total revenue typically originates from the Primary Catchment (within 1.5 km or 8 minutes walk) in an urban retail franchise?**
   - A) 10% – 20%
   - B) 30% – 40%
   - C) 60% – 70%
   - D) 90% – 100%
   - **Correct Answer:** **C**
   - **Explanation:** In urban retail and food service, the immediate primary catchment accounts for 60% to 70% of total customer transactions due to walking proximity and routine convenience.

2. **If an outlet projects ₹6,00,000 in monthly net sales, what is the maximum recommended total monthly occupancy cost (Rent + CAM) under the 15% rule for F&B?**
   - A) ₹1,50,000
   - B) ₹90,000
   - C) ₹45,000
   - D) ₹1,20,000
   - **Correct Answer:** **B**
   - **Explanation:** $₹6,00,000 \times 15\% = ₹90,000.00$. Rent and CAM exceeding ₹90,000 will compress operating margins into an unsustainable deficit.
