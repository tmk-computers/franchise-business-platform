## 1. Valuation Fundamentals: SDE vs. EBITDA

Valuing a franchised business differs fundamentally from valuing an independent enterprise. Because the incoming buyer must operate within the franchisor’s pricing, royalty structure, and supply chain constraints, enterprise value is tied directly to the **normalized cash flow** generated by that specific location.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             SDE VS. EBITDA IN VALUATION                               |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                       |
|   SELLER'S DISCRETIONARY EARNINGS (SDE)       EARNINGS BEFORE INTEREST, TAXES,        |
|   (Used for single-unit FOFO franchises       DEPRECIATION & AMORTIZATION (EBITDA)    |
|   where owner works full-time on-site)        (Used for multi-unit portfolios run     |
|                                                by salaried General Managers)          |
|                                                                                       |
|   SDE = Net Accounting Profit                 EBITDA = Net Operating Profit           |
|       + Owner's Full Salary/Draws                    + Depreciation                   |
|       + Non-Operating Personal Perks                 + Amortization                   |
|       + Non-Recurring One-Time Expenses              + Interest Expenses              |
|       + Depreciation & Amortization                  (Includes full GM salaries)      |
|                                                                                       |
|   Typical Multiples: 1.8x - 2.8x SDE          Typical Multiples: 3.5x - 5.5x EBITDA   |
|                                                                                       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### The SDE Method (Single Units)
In a single-unit FOFO franchise, the owner often draws personal benefits—such as personal vehicle fuel, mobile bills, personal health insurance, or a below-market salary. SDE reconstitutes these items to show an incoming individual buyer how much total cash they will pocket annually if they take over the owner-operator role.

### The EBITDA Method (Multi-Unit Portfolios)
Institutional buyers, family offices, and private equity investors do not intend to stand behind the counter. They value the business based on **EBITDA after paying full-time market-rate store managers**. Because multi-unit platforms possess professional middle management and lower owner-keyman risk, they trade at significantly higher valuation multiples.

## 2. Normalizing the P&L: The Add-Back Schedule

To present a credible valuation to serious buyers, the seller must prepare a documented **Add-Back Schedule** supported by bank statements and audited tax returns.

```python
# Worked Add-Back Normalization Model (Single-Unit QSR Cafe)
Reported_Accounting_Net_Profit = 780000.00

# Legitimate Add-Back Adjustments
Owner_Direct_Salary = 480000.00
Personal_Vehicle_Fuel_Expense = 60000.00
Personal_Mobile_Internet = 24000.00
One_Time_Kitchen_Civil_Repair = 75000.00
Accounting_Depreciation_Equipment = 110000.00

# Discretionary Cash Flow (SDE)
Total_Add_Backs = (Owner_Direct_Salary + 
                   Personal_Vehicle_Fuel_Expense + 
                   Personal_Mobile_Internet + 
                   One_Time_Kitchen_Civil_Repair + 
                   Accounting_Depreciation_Equipment) # 749,000.00

True_Normalized_SDE = Reported_Accounting_Net_Profit + Total_Add_Backs # 1,529,000.00
```

| Line Item | Reported P&L (₹) | Adjustment (₹) | Normalized P&L (₹) | Rationale for Adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Customer Revenue** | ₹96,00,000 | — | ₹96,00,000 | Verified through cloud POS sales logs |
| *Less: Statutory GST (18%)* | *(₹14,64,407)*| — | *(₹14,64,407)*| Net revenue = ₹81,35,593 |
| Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) | (₹26,03,390)| — | (₹26,03,390)| 32% food and packaging costs |
| Commercial Property Rent | (₹14,40,000)| — | (₹14,40,000)| Fixed lease: ₹1,20,000/month |
| Staff Payroll (excl. owner) | (₹10,20,000)| — | (₹10,20,000)| Frontline staff wages |
| Power, Water & Utilities | (₹4,20,000) | — | (₹4,20,000) | Commercial utilities |
| Franchisor Royalty & Ad Fund| (₹6,50,847) | — | (₹6,50,847) | 8% combined royalty on net revenue |
| Owner Salary Draw | (₹4,80,000) | **+₹4,80,000** | ₹0 | **Add-Back:** Discretionary owner draw |
| Personal Vehicle Fuel | (₹60,000) | **+₹60,000** | ₹0 | **Add-Back:** Personal owner perk |
| Personal Mobile & Broadband| (₹24,000) | **+₹24,000** | ₹0 | **Add-Back:** Personal utility expensed |
| One-Time Plumbing Overhaul | (₹75,000) | **+₹75,000** | ₹0 | **Add-Back:** Non-recurring capital repair |
| Equipment Depreciation | (₹1,10,000) | **+₹1,10,000** | ₹0 | **Add-Back:** Non-cash accounting entry |
| Miscellaneous Operating | (₹91,949) | — | (₹91,949) | Consumables and local marketing |
| **TOTALS** | **₹7,80,000** | **+₹7,49,000** | **₹15,29,000**| **True Normalized SDE = ₹15,29,000.00** |

At a standard market multiple of **2.2x SDE**, this business has an estimated enterprise value of:

$$\text{Enterprise Value} = ₹15,29,000 \times 2.2 = ₹33,63,800.00$$

Without the normalized add-back schedule, a naive buyer would have offered 2.2x on the reported ₹7,80,000 accounting profit (only ₹17,16,000), shortchanging the seller by more than ₹16 Lakhs!

## 3. The Impact of Remaining Lease and Agreement Life

A franchise business does not exist in perpetuity—its economic lifespan is strictly capped by two legal instruments:
1. The **Remaining Term of the Franchise Agreement**.
2. The **Remaining Term of the Commercial Real Estate Lease**.

If a franchise agreement has only 18 months remaining before expiration, and the franchisor charges a ₹5,00,000 renewal fee plus demands a mandatory ₹10,00,000 store remodel upon renewal, a buyer will dramatically discount their offer.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                    TERM-LENGTH VALUATION DISCOUNT SCHEDULE                            |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Remaining Unexpired Term (Lease & Franchise)  |  Valuation Adjustment Factor         |
|  --------------------------------------------  |  ---------------------------         |
|  More than 5 Years Remaining                   |  100% of Base Multiple (Full Value)  |
|  3 to 5 Years Remaining                        |  85% - 90% of Base Multiple          |
|  2 to 3 Years Remaining                        |  70% - 80% of Base Multiple          |
|  Less than 2 Years Remaining                   |  Liquidated Asset Value ONLY         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ### 💡 The Golden Rule of Timing an Exit
> Always initiate your franchise exit sale when you have **at least 3 to 4 years remaining** on your franchise agreement and commercial lease. Selling with less than 24 months remaining destroys your negotiating leverage.

## 5. Investor Warning Signs in Franchise Resales

> [!WARNING]
> ### 🚨 Red Flags When Buying a Franchise Resale
> 1. **"Cash-in-Drawer Off-the-Books Sales" Claims:** A seller claiming the store earns an extra ₹50,000/week in unrecorded cash sales. Never pay a valuation multiple on unverified, off-the-books cash flow.
> 2. **Imminent Mandatory Store Remodel:** The seller fails to disclose that the franchisor has mandated an architectural renovation within the next 6 months costing ₹12 Lakhs.
> 3. **Non-Assignable Commercial Lease:** The landlord refuses to assign the commercial lease to the incoming buyer, or demands a 50% rental escalation as a condition of consenting to the transfer.

## 6. Key Takeaways

1. **SDE for Single Units; EBITDA for Portfolios:** Match the valuation methodology to the organizational maturity and owner involvement of the business.
2. **Normalize Legitimate Add-Backs:** Transparently add back owner salary, personal perks, non-cash depreciation, and one-off extraordinary repairs to reveal true discretionary cash flow.
3. **Protect Remaining Term Length:** Never let your franchise agreement or commercial lease drop below 3 years if you plan to extract maximum exit value.
4. **Scrutinize Franchisor Transfer Conditions:** Ensure you know the franchisor’s transfer fee and buyer qualification standards before marketing the business.

## 7. Chapter Knowledge Check

1. **When calculating Seller’s Discretionary Earnings (SDE), which of the following is considered a legitimate add-back to accounting net profit?**
   - A) Regular commercial rent paid to the landlord
   - B) Standard raw material ingredient purchases
   - C) Non-cash accounting equipment depreciation and discretionary owner salary draws
   - D) Ongoing monthly franchisor royalties
   - **Correct Answer:** **C**
   - **Explanation:** SDE reconstitutes non-cash accounting charges (depreciation) and personal owner compensation draws, showing the total unencumbered operating cash flow available to a new full-time owner-operator.

2. **Why do franchise units with less than 2 years remaining on their franchise agreement and commercial lease trade at severe valuation discounts?**
   - A) Customers stop visiting stores after 3 years
   - B) The incoming buyer faces imminent renewal fees, landlord lease renegotiation risks, and mandatory corporate remodeling capital costs
   - C) GST taxes double after 3 years
   - D) POS software stops working after 2 years
   - **Correct Answer:** **B**
   - **Explanation:** Short remaining term shifts massive capital risk (remodeling CapEx, renewal fees, lease expiration) directly onto the incoming buyer, destroying business goodwill value.
